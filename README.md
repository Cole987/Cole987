## 01 — Who I Am

I'm a political professional who writes code in my spare time.

My world revolves around political communication, electoral campaigns, institutions and the occasional existential question about democracy.

When I'm not working on political projects, I'm probably building an application, experimenting with a server, reviving old software or wondering why something that worked perfectly yesterday has decided to become a constitutional crisis today.

I enjoy understanding complex systems — whether they're political institutions, computer operating systems or international power dynamics.

**I'm a politician who codes. I believe in public service, open-mindedness and occasionally blaming the compiler.**

*My code has no political affiliation. Its bugs, however, refuse to cooperate with the government.*

## 02 — What I Do

### 🏛️ Politics & Political Communication

Political strategy, electoral campaigns, communication and institutions are central to my professional interests.

I enjoy turning ideas into concrete action, organising projects, communicating political messages and understanding how institutions work.

In politics, getting everyone to agree on a sentence can be harder than debugging a program.

At least a compiler tells you when it disagrees.

### 💻 Software & Technology

I build personal projects, develop applications and explore the systems behind the technology we use every day.

My interests range from Swift and SwiftUI to Python, web technologies, servers and legacy operating systems.

I enjoy the challenge of making things work — including things that were originally designed when some of today's developers were still learning to walk.

### 🌍 Beyond Both

History, geopolitics and political institutions help me understand the world. Formula 1 reminds me that strategy is everything — and that even the best plan can fall apart before the first corner.

Apparently, that's true in politics too.

### `03` · SystemBanner_XP

**Because classified information deserves retro compatibility.**

A fork of SystemBanner, an application designed to display computer classification markings in accordance with the framework of U.S. Executive Order 13526.

I ported the application across a rather ambitious range of operating systems:

- **Windows:** 2000, XP, Vista and 7.
- **Mac OS X / macOS:** From Snow Leopard to modern macOS releases.
- **Apple Silicon:** An ARM version built in C#, with help from Claude.

The goal was to bring classification-banner functionality to systems spanning multiple generations of desktop computing — from the Windows 2000 era to modern ARM-based Macs.

Some people collect vintage computers. I give them administrative responsibilities.

**Technologies:** C# · Windows · macOS · ARM

[View SystemBanner_XP →](https://github.com/Cole987/SystemBanner_XP)

## 06 — A Few Principles

- **Understand before building.** The best solutions start with the right questions.
- **Make old things useful.** Legacy doesn't have to mean useless.
- **Stay curious.** There is always another system to understand.
- **Document your work.** Future you is another developer, and future you deserves better.
- **Never trust a bug-free program.** Either you're exceptionally talented, or you haven't tested it properly.
